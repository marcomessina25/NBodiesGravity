# PR: NBodiesGravity v1.0.1 — Dynamic System Boundaries, Escape Handling & Dynamical Stability Diagnostics

## Overview

This pull request delivers **v1.0.1** of NBodiesGravity, completing all objectives and acceptance criteria defined in [`docs/specs/v101.md`](docs/specs/v101.md).

Version 1.0.1 removes the artificial simulation halt caused by bodies escaping the solar system, establishes dynamic system boundaries with physical ejection handling, introduces transient non-modal warning alerts, and equips the scientific diagnostics laboratory with real-time dynamical stability diagnostics based on peer-reviewed celestial mechanics criteria.

---

## Scientific Rationale & Motivation

In gravitational N-body systems, adding stellar companions or massive perturbing bodies routinely precipitates close three-body encounters and gravitational slingshots. These dynamical interactions naturally impart positive specific orbital energy ($E \ge 0$) to lower-mass bodies, placing them on hyperbolic escape trajectories ($e \ge 1, v \ge v_{\text{esc}}$).

In prior versions (v1.0.0), any body drifting beyond $r = 1000\text{ AU}$ was indiscriminately treated as a catastrophic numerical failure, triggering an abrupt simulation pause (`auto_paused_blowup`). This design suffered from two key scientific and usability shortcomings:
1. **Conflation of Numerical Breakdown and Physical Escape**: Genuine numerical blow-up (division-by-zero singularities, non-finite `NaN`/`Inf` floating-point states) was conflated with physically legitimate hyperbolic ejections.
2. **Computational Waste & Force Decoupling**: Because gravitational force decays as $1/r^2$, a body at $1000\text{ AU}$ from a $1\,M_\odot$ primary experiences an acceleration $10^{-6}$ times smaller than at $1\text{ AU}$, and its reciprocal back-reaction on the inner solar system is completely negligible ($\sim 10^{-12}$ to $10^{-18}$). Continuing to integrate far-away escaping bodies in $O(N^2)$ force summation degrades performance without contributing physical fidelity.

v1.0.1 addresses this by treating the system as dynamically open with respect to unbounded escapees while preserving strict symplectic numerical integrity for the remaining bound system.

## Key Workstreams & Implemented Features

### 1. Dynamic System Boundaries & Hyperbolic Escape Pruning (`nbodiesgravity/engine/stability.py`, `system.py`)
- **`EscapeConfig` & Physical Ejection Detection**: Defined explicit escape parameters (default: $r_{\text{threshold}} = 1000.0\text{ AU}$, $E_{\text{threshold}} = 0.0$). Unbound bodies exceeding the threshold are cleanly extracted via `_resolve_ejections()`.
- **$O(N^2)$ Computational Pruning**: Ejected bodies are removed from active integration and future rendering snapshots, immediately lightening the pairwise force computation load.
- **Ejection Event Tracking**: Complete ejection history is recorded in `SolarSystem.ejection_history` (`EjectionEvent`), logging the body's name, mass, radius, color, ejection timestamp, exit position, velocity magnitude, and asymptotic hyperbolic excess velocity $v_\infty = \sqrt{v^2 - 2GM/r}$.
- **Conservation Tracker Re-baselining**: The active energy and momentum conservation baseline is automatically re-baselined upon body ejection, preventing artificial conservation alerts for the remaining bound subsystem.

### 2. Strict Separation of Numerical Blow-up vs. Physical Ejection (`nbodiesgravity/engine/simulation_thread.py`)
- **Finite-State Verification**: The simulation thread maintains strict numerical integrity checks, immediately pausing on non-finite coordinates, velocities, or accelerations (`NaN`/`Inf`).
- **Continuous Simulation**: Physical ejections emit the `ejections_detected` Qt signal and allow the physics simulation loop to proceed uninterrupted.

### 3. Non-Modal Ejection Alerts & Camera Safety (`nbodiesgravity/ui/ejection_warning_dialog.py`, `main_window.py`)
- **`EjectionWarningDialog`**: Transient, non-modal notification dialog displayed on ejection. Displays the escaping body name, distance from barycenter, speed, and time. Features an automatic 4-second countdown and a manual "Dismiss" button.
- **Automatic Camera & Trail Recovery**: If an escaping body was currently selected as the camera focus target or reference frame center body, `MainWindow._on_ejections` safely resets the camera to the primary star (Sun) and purges out-of-frame trails, preventing viewport disorientation.

### 4. Real-Time Dynamical Stability Diagnostics (`nbodiesgravity/engine/stability.py`, `ui/diagnostics_dialog.py`)
- **Gladman (1993) Hill Stability**: Evaluates mutual Hill radius separations $\Delta = (a_2 - a_1) / R_H$ against the analytic stability threshold:
  $$\Delta > 2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.464$$
  Flagging systems as dynamically stable or unstable against close planetary encounters.
- **Holman & Wiegert (1999) Binary Stability Boundaries**:
  - **S-Type (Circumprimary)**: Evaluates critical survival radius $a_c$ around primary stars in binary systems ($a_c = a_{\text{bin}} [0.464 - 0.380\mu - 0.631e + 0.586\mu e + 0.150e^2 - 0.198\mu e^2]$).
  - **P-Type (Circumbinary)**: Evaluates critical inner stability boundary for planets orbiting both stars ($a_c = a_{\text{bin}} [1.60 + 5.10e - 2.22e^2 + 4.12\mu - 4.27e\mu - 5.09\mu^2 + 4.61e^2\mu^2]$).
- **Geometric Orbit-Crossing**: Flags crossing orbits ($r_{p, outer} \le r_{a, inner}$) where secular resonance or close collisions can arise.
- **UI Diagnostics Integration**: `ScientificDiagnosticsDialog` (`Ctrl+D`) includes a dedicated "Dynamical Stability & System Integrity" group displaying real-time system status badges (`STABLE`, `UNSTABLE (ORBIT CROSSING)`, `UNSTABLE (HILL CRITERION)`, `CRITICAL (BINARY TIDE)`), active configuration metrics, and comprehensive ejection audit tables.
