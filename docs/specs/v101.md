## 1. Motivation & Scientific Problem Statement

In previous versions of NBodiesGravity (v0.5.0 through v1.0.0), a hard threshold of $1,000\text{ AU}$ was enforced on active body coordinates. If any active body expanded past $1,000\text{ AU}$, the simulation loop halted with a generic "Blow-Up Detected" message and paused execution.

From an astrophysical and simulation design standpoint, this behavior was fundamentally flawed:
1. **Physical Ejections vs. Numerical Blow-Ups**:
   - Adding stellar companions (binary stars), stellar flybys, or chaotic multi-planet close encounters routinely leads to the ejection of smaller bodies (planets, asteroids, comets) on hyperbolic escape trajectories ($E \ge 0$).
   - Halting the simulation treated a legitimate astrophysical phenomenon as a computational crash.
2. **Computational & Numerical Degradation of Distant Escapees**:
   - Retaining an escaped body thousands of AU away forces the $O(N^2)$ integrator to evaluate forces that decay as $1/r^2 < 10^{-6}$, wasting compute cycles.
   - Coordinate ranges spanning from $10^{-3}\text{ AU}$ (lunar orbits) to $10^4\text{ AU}$ degrade floating-point cancellation precision and compress the inner system into a single pixel in 3D OpenGL rendering.
3. **Absence of Real-Time Stability Indicators**:
   - When a user inserts an additional star or alters orbital semi-major axes, the user lacked real-time scientific feedback on whether the perturbed configuration was dynamically stable or bound for chaotic disruption.

## 2. Core Architectural & Algorithmic Enhancements

### 2.1 Physics Engine & Ejection Mechanics (`nbodiesgravity.engine.stability`)
- **`EscapeConfig`**: Configurable boundary parameters (`min_distance_au = 1000.0`, `extent_factor = 5.0`).
- **`find_ejected_index`**: Identifies escaped bodies meeting four simultaneous criteria:
  1. $r > r_{\rm min}$ ($1,000\text{ AU}$ from remaining system barycenter).
  2. $r \ge 5.0 \times R_{\rm extent}$ or $r \ge 2,000\text{ AU}$ (monopole regime).
  3. $\mathbf{r}_{\rm rel} \cdot \mathbf{v}_{\rm rel} \ge 0$ (moving outward).
  4. Specific mechanical energy $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2} v^2 - \frac{G M_{\rm rest}}{\sqrt{r^2 + \varepsilon_{\rm soft}^2}} \ge 0$ (unbound hyperbolic escape).
- **`EjectionEvent`**: Records escape parameters including asymptotic hyperbolic excess velocity $v_\infty = \sqrt{2 \varepsilon}$.
- **`SolarSystem` Ejection Lifecycle**:
  - `_resolve_ejections()` removes the escaped body from `_bodies` (excluding it from future integration steps).
  - Deactivates the body (`active = False`) and records it in `ejected_bodies` and `ejection_history`.
  - Staging queue `pop_recent_ejections()` allows thread-safe signal dispatching without polling locks.

### 2.2 Numerical Integrity Decoupling (`nbodiesgravity.engine.simulation_thread`)
- `blow_up_detected` is strictly reserved for genuine mathematical singularities ($\text{NaN}$ or $\pm\infty$).
- Distance expansion beyond $1,000\text{ AU}$ triggers `ejections_detected`, which automatically excludes the body while the simulation thread **continues running smoothly without pausing**.
- When an ejection occurs, the conservation tracker re-baselines to the remaining active bodies so future drift evaluations continue measuring numerical integration error accurately.

### 2.3 Non-Modal Auto-Closing Notification (`nbodiesgravity.ui.ejection_warning_dialog`)
- **`EjectionWarningDialog`**:
  - Non-modal presentation (`setModal(False)`): does not freeze the UI or the background simulation thread.
  - Automatically closes in 4 seconds using a live countdown timer (`QTimer`).
  - Displays body name, boundary distance, and asymptotic escape speed $v_\infty$.
  - Batches multiple ejections gracefully via `append_events()`, resetting the countdown without dialog spam.
- **Camera Retargeting**:
  - If the camera was locked onto an ejected body, `MainWindow._on_ejections` automatically retargets to the Sun or dominant primary and clears orbital trails.

### 2.4 Dynamical Stability Diagnostics
- **Gladman (1993) Mutual Hill Separation**:
  $$\Delta = \frac{a_2 - a_1}{R_{H,m}}, \quad R_{H,m} = \left(\frac{m_1 + m_2}{3 M_*}\right)^{1/3} \frac{a_1 + a_2}{2}$$
  - $\Delta > 2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.46$: Hill-stable.
  - $\Delta < 2\sqrt{3}$: Hill-unstable.
  - $3.46 \le \Delta < 5.0$: Marginal.
- **Holman & Wiegert (1999) Critical Semi-Major Axes**:
  - S-type (circumprimary) critical radius $a_c$.
  - P-type (circumbinary) critical radius $a_c$.
- **Orbit-Crossing Test**: $Q_1 \ge q_2$ triggers Unstable warning.
- **Diagnostics UI**: Real-time badges (`STABLE`, `MARGINAL`, `UNSTABLE`), minimum mutual Hill separation, primary star identification, and explicit scientific findings.
